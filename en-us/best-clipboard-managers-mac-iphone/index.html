Clipboard managers for Mac that work with your iPhone

Six apps and what macOS does on its own, by what each keeps on your Mac and what reaches your iPhone.

Cutling gives you a clipboard history on the Mac and puts the snippets you save in a keyboard on your iPhone and iPad, for $4.99, paid once once. Paste and PastePal sync your whole history to the iPhone instead; Maccy, ClipBook and Raycast keep theirs on the Mac. I make Cutling, and every claim about the others links to the page it came from.

Cutling

Cutling is for the things you save on purpose. On iPhone and iPad they sit in a keyboard. On a Mac, ⌘⇧V opens a picker at your pointer with the same cutlings and a history of what you copied, and with direct paste on, your pick goes straight into the app you were in. Saved cutlings sync through your own iCloud. The history stays on the Mac and skips anything a password manager marks as concealed. Cutling is $4.99, paid once on the App Store with the Mac app included, and the Mac app is also free as a direct download. Its privacy label says Data Not Collected.

Paste

Paste is built around one history: everything you copy on any device lands in one history that syncs through iCloud, its search reads the text inside screenshots, Pinboards can be shared, and on iPhone the Paste Keyboard brings the whole history into any app. On a Mac, ⌘1 to 9 pastes recent items and Paste Stack pastes a queue in order. It is $3.99 a month, $29.99 a year, $59.99 a year for a family or $89.99 once, after a one-week trial. Its privacy label lists Identifiers as data linked to you.

PastePal

The closest thing to Paste that you pay for once. Your history syncs through your own iCloud if you turn it on, and on iPhone there is a keyboard extension, an iMessage extension and widgets. On the Mac it has a side window with ⌘1 to 9, a paste queue, and text recognition from any part of the screen. The app is free to download, but pasting needs the $7.99 Pro purchase, which covers Mac, iPhone and iPad and works with Family Sharing. Its privacy label says Data Not Collected.

Raycast

A launcher for the Mac whose Clipboard History is free: text, images, colors and links, encrypted on your Mac, kept for 3 months on the free plan and without a limit on Pro. The history never leaves the Mac. Raycast for iOS needs a free account, and with Pro, at $10 a month or $96 a year, it syncs your Raycast Snippets to the iPhone, where its keyboard can insert them. The current Mac version needs macOS Tahoe on Apple silicon, and an older version is still offered for download.

Maccy

Free, open source under the MIT license, and Mac only. It keeps your copy history, searches it as you type, and stores it all on your Mac. The same app is $9.99 on the Mac App Store, sold to support its development. There is no iPhone app and no sync.

ClipBook

Mac only, $14.99 once for one Mac, $24.99 for two or $49.99 for five, with a 5-day trial. It is built for heavy keyboard use: ⌃V pastes history items one after another, 78 text transformations, and copy-and-merge. Its own comparison with Paste lists no iPhone app and no iCloud sync.

What macOS and iOS do on their own

Universal Clipboard lets you copy on one Apple device and paste on another, when both are near each other, signed in to the same Apple Account, with Bluetooth, Wi-Fi and Handoff on. It carries one item, which stays briefly or until you copy something else.

On macOS Tahoe 26, Spotlight keeps a clipboard history. Press ⌘Space, then ⌘4, and you can search it, copy an item again or clear it. Apple's guide says nothing about getting that history onto an iPhone.

How this list was made

Five of these apps come up most in search results and AI answers for a Mac clipboard manager in November 2026. The sixth, Cutling, is mine. Prices and features come from each app's App Store listing and website on 20 November 2026, linked above.
