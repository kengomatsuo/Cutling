## How many frames show in search

Apple, "Creating your product page": "Depending on the orientation of your screenshots, the first
one to three images will appear in search results when no app preview is available."
Apple, "Search" on the App Store pages for developers: "up to three screenshots or app previews
may display in search results depending on the platform and image orientation."

So three portrait frames side by side is the most a search result shows, it is not promised on
every platform, and an app preview video takes one of the places. Cutling's iPhone listing must
have no app preview in front of these frames for the strip to show whole.

## Techniques

| # | Technique | Who does it | Cutling | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One picture across frames 1 to 3 | Paste (iPhone and Mac), Maccy, Copied, WordBoard, PastePal (Mac) | Used | It is what the owner asked for, and three frames are what search shows |
| 2 | A device crossing a seam | Paste (MacBook over two seams, iPad into the third frame), PastePal (Mac) | Drawn as S2, not recommended | The crossing phone has to be cropped by its own frame, and a cropped phone is what the owner rejected |
| 3 | A card or tile crossing a seam | Paste (the tilted Link card; on Mac a whole shelf of cards), Maccy (callouts) | Used | The app's own tiles are the thing sold; two of them cross the two seams |
| 4 | A conversation crossing a seam | Copied (bubbles in frame 1, the phone in frame 2) | Rejected | It leaves frame 1 with no device and no product |
| 5 | Enlarged pieces lifted out of the screen | Paste frames 3 to 7, SwiftKey every frame, TextExpander, WordBoard, Copied, Maccy | Used | The two lifted tiles are real capture pixels at capture size, half again as large as in the phone |
| 6 | Tilted or perspective phone | Paste Keyboard, PastePal, WordBoard (held in a hand) | Rejected | The interface text stops being readable at search size, and every tilted rival also crops the phone |
| 7 | Phone bleeding off the canvas, or no phone at all | AutoPaste, SwiftKey, Paste Keyboard, WordBoard, 1Blocker | Rejected for the recommended strip | Owner: the whole phone. S2 shows what it would look like |
| 8 | More than one kind of device | Paste (Mac, iPad), PastePal | Rejected in frames 1 to 3 | This is the iPhone listing and the first three frames sell the keyboard; a Mac belongs in a later frame |
| 9 | Award laurels | Paste, PastePal, WordBoard, TextExpander ("Trusted by over 100k users") | Rejected | Nothing on disk to quote |
| 10 | Review quotes as a frame | WordBoard frame 6 | Rejected | No reviews on disk to quote |
| 11 | A small coloured label above the headline (KEEP, SEARCH) | Paste frames 2 to 8, a new colour each | Rejected | The green phrase in each headline does that job; a label would be a second accent |
| 12 | A new ground colour per frame | PastePal, TextExpander (white, orange, dark) | Rejected | One ground is what lets three frames read as one picture; Paste keeps one ground too |
| 13 | One coloured or boxed phrase in the headline | AutoPaste, WordBoard | Used | One green phrase per headline: one tap, one place, any app |
| 14 | A second, quieter line under the headline | 1Blocker, Paste Keyboard, PastePal (Mac) | Used | It says how, in a line that still reads at search size |
| 15 | A touch mark or a hand | Paste (white dot), Copied and TextExpander (cursor hand), WordBoard and SwiftKey (drawn hand) | Adapted | One green mark on the key that was tapped; no hand |
| 16 | A staged message that asks the question | AutoPaste ("What's your email?"), Copied, WordBoard, Clipboard - Paste Keyboard ("Can you tell me your shipping address?") | Used | One incoming bubble, "What's your address?", fills the empty chat and gives the paste a reason |
| 17 | Arrows | SwiftKey, WordBoard | Rejected | Each tile sits beside the place it was pasted, which says the same without a drawn arrow |
| 18 | Icon and name inside the frame | TextExpander, SwiftKey | Rejected | The store shows both beside the screenshots |
| 19 | A wall of other companies' app icons | TextExpander frame 5 | Rejected | Other companies' marks |
| 20 | Trophies, emoji stickers, comic bursts | PastePal, SwiftKey, AutoPaste | Rejected | They break the calm the owner chose |
| 21 | A dark frame to close the set | TextExpander | Not in frames 1 to 3 | A candidate for the last frame |
| 22 | Bare screenshots with no words | Clipboard - Paste Keyboard | Rejected | Nothing reads at search size |

## How the story runs, frame by frame

| App | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 and on |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paste | The promise, with awards | Keep | Search | Organise | Keyboard, widget, share, sync |
| WordBoard | A claim ("Type 10x faster") | Use it | Make keys | Pictures | Forms, then reviews |
| AutoPaste | A gimmick | What it is | Add text | Paste in one tap | A second gimmick |
| TextExpander | The promise | Forms | Suggestions | The library | Everywhere, settings, dark |
| Cutling | The result: your address in one tap | The library: all you retype in one place | The reach: your sign-off in any app | not drawn | not drawn |

Paste spends frame 1 on a slogan and its awards. Cutling has no awards, so frame 1 shows the
result happening instead, and the second and third frames answer the two questions it raises:
where the text lives, and where it works.
