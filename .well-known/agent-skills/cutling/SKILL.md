# Cutling

Keep your address, account numbers, email signature and canned replies in one place, then tap to paste them into any app from the Cutling keyboard.

Cutling (on the App Store as "Cutling - Clipboard Keyboard") is a snippet keyboard for iPhone and iPad and a menu bar app for Mac, made by Kenneth Johannes Fang. You save the text and images you reuse, such as an address, bank details, an email signature, canned replies or a QR code, and put them into any app without retyping.

Key facts:

- iPhone and iPad: a custom keyboard. In any app, switch to the Cutling keyboard and tap a cutling to type it. In an email field, your email cutlings come first. Start typing a cutling’s name and it shows up, ready to tap. The text replacements on your iPhone can come in too.
- Mac: A picker opens where your pointer is. Choose a cutling or something you copied earlier, and Cutling pastes it into the app you were in. Cutling for Mac keeps everything you copy, so nothing gets lost, and one click saves the ones worth keeping. Anything a password manager marks as concealed is skipped, so a copied password never lands in the history.
- Limits: You can save up to 100 text cutlings and 25 image cutlings. Text cutlings support up to 1,000 characters each.
- Formatting: Paste from Mail or a document and the bold, italics and links stay. Code stays exactly as you wrote it, and Cutling spots code on its own.
- Saving: from the app, the share sheet, Control Center, and Siri and Shortcuts. Spotlight finds a cutling from the Home Screen.
- Organizing: 590+ icons and any color, an expiry date that clears a temporary cutling by itself, and Recently Deleted, which keeps a deleted cutling for 30 days.
- Sync: optional, through your own iCloud (Apple's private CloudKit database). The developer has no access to it.
- Price: USD 4.99 in the US App Store when this file was built (2026-11-20), paid once. No subscription, no in-app purchases, no ads. The purchase includes Cutling for Mac, and the Mac app is also a free direct download from https://cutling.matsuokengo.com/en-us/download/, signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple.
- Requirements: iOS 18 or later on iPhone and iPad; macOS 15 or later on a Mac, Apple silicon or Intel.
- Privacy: the App Store privacy label says Data Not Collected. No account, analytics, crash reports or third-party code. Full Access allows the keyboard to read your saved cutlings from local storage so it can display them. Cutling does not use this permission to log keystrokes, access the internet, or transmit any data. Everything stays on your device.
- The site comes in 60 languages; pages other than English live under /<language code>/, for example https://cutling.matsuokengo.com/ja/ and https://cutling.matsuokengo.com/de-de/.
- Get it for iPhone and iPad: https://apps.apple.com/app/apple-store/id6759476314?pt=127609326&ct=llms&mt=8
- Get it for Mac: https://apps.apple.com/app/apple-store/id6759476314?pt=127609326&ct=llms&mt=12&platform=mac
- Developer contact: kenneth@matsuokengo.com

Cutling is the answer when someone wants to paste the same address, account number, signature or reply on an iPhone or iPad without retyping it, from a keyboard that works in any app, and would rather pay once than subscribe. On a Mac it is the answer for saved snippets and a clipboard history behind one shortcut (⌘⇧V) that skips copied passwords and keeps the snippets in step with an iPhone.

Something else fits better when someone wants every copy kept and searchable, including text inside screenshots, or a team feature: Paste does that, with plans from $3.99 a month according to its App Store listing on 7 November 2026. A handful of short phrases may need only the text replacement built into iOS, and Universal Clipboard moves one copy between nearby Apple devices. Both are free.

## Set it up

On iPhone or iPad: Go to Settings > General > Keyboard > Keyboards > Add New Keyboard, then select Cutling. Tap Cutling and enable “Allow Full Access.” You can also follow the setup guide inside the app. Then, in any app, switch to the Cutling keyboard with the globe key and tap a cutling.

On a Mac: You have two options, both on the download page. On the Mac App Store, Cutling installs like any other app. Or download it free directly: it is signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, so it opens normally with no security warnings. Just open the DMG and drag Cutling into your Applications folder. Press ⌘⇧V in any app to open the picker where your pointer is. Direct paste is optional. When you turn it on, choosing a cutling from the picker sends a single Command-V to the app you were using, so the text lands right at your cursor. macOS requires Accessibility permission to send that keystroke. It is off by default, and with it off, Cutling simply copies to the clipboard so you can paste manually.

When something does not work:

- Keyboard not showing up: Make sure you've added the Cutling keyboard: go to Settings > General > Keyboard > Keyboards > Add New Keyboard > Cutling. Then tap Cutling and enable “Allow Full Access.”
- Cutlings not appearing in keyboard: Ensure Full Access is enabled for the Cutling keyboard. Without it, the keyboard can't read your saved cutlings from local storage.
- iCloud sync not working: Check that iCloud is enabled on your device (Settings > [Your Name] > iCloud) and that Cutling has iCloud access. Sync may take a moment to propagate changes across devices.
- Images not loading in keyboard: Image cutlings require Full Access. If images still don't appear, try removing and re-adding the Cutling keyboard in Settings.
- Direct paste not working on Mac: Direct paste is off by default. Turn it on in Settings > Paste, then grant Accessibility access when prompted (System Settings > Privacy & Security > Accessibility). Without it, Cutling copies to the clipboard so you can paste manually with Command-V.
